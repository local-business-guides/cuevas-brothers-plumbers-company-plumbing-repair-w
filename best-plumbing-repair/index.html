Compare the work, timing, and terms

A repair typically starts with an assessment of the reported symptoms. The plumber may inspect the relevant fixture, accessible piping, or drain before explaining a possible solution. Confirm whether diagnosis and repair are separate steps, and ask before approving additional work if the findings change.

Key facts for comparing options:
- Scope: Ask which part or system is included, what is not included, and what condition would lead to a revised recommendation.
- Timing: A straightforward, accessible issue may be easier to assess than one with an uncertain source or limited access. Parts and the amount of work can also affect when a repair is completed. Ask for an estimate based on the actual inspection.
- Cost factors: The cause, the extent of the work, access, parts, and labor may affect the total. Request an explanation of the estimate and any separate charges before agreeing to proceed.

Useful terms include diagnosis, the process of identifying a problem; scope, the work included in a proposal; and shutoff valve, a valve used to stop water flow to a line or fixture. If a term is unfamiliar, ask the plumber to explain it in everyday language.
